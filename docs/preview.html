🔬 Lab 1 — TTA playground (try it!)

A normal CPU orders operations. A TTA orders moves: writing to a trigger port fires the unit. With B buses you finish M moves in ceil(M/B) cycles.

Buses B: 2 Moves M: 3

Cycles needed: 2

ADD on this chip: 3 moves on 2 buses = 2 cycles. At 20 MHz that is 40M moves/s.

Exposed bypass (the dividend)

Chained multiply-then-add: normal CPUs write the middle answer to a register and read it back (4 reads + 2 writes). TTA wires MUL.result → ALU.operand directly: saves ≥2 register accesses. Less traffic, less energy — the compiler does the routing the hardware used to do.

⏱ Lab 2 — Timing: why the MAC almost broke the chip

At 20 MHz every answer must be ready in 50 ns. Drag the pipeline stages: more stages = shorter each = easier to close, at the cost of a few cycles of delay (throughput stays 1/cycle).

Pipeline stages: 1 (combinational)

Stage delay: 45.0 ns → ❌ violates slow corner (52 ns > 50 ns)

Model: 32-bit multiplier 30–40 ns + 64-bit add 8–12 ns (GF180, FO4-calibrated). Margin 2 ns.
